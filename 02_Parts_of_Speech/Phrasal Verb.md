# Phrasal Verbs — IELTS Academic Master Guide

> [!abstract] Learning objective
> Understand and use phrasal verbs accurately in IELTS Speaking, Reading, Listening, and, where appropriate, Academic Writing.
>
> **Core principle:** Natural English is better than forced sophistication.

## 01. What is a phrasal verb?

A phrasal verb is formed by combining a verb with one or more particles, usually an adverb or a preposition.

The combination may have a meaning that is different from the original verb.

**Examples**

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Give up | Stop doing something | I gave up drinking coffee. |
| Look after | Take care of | She looks after her younger brother. |
| Put off | Postpone | We put off the meeting. |
| Carry out | Perform or conduct | Researchers carried out an experiment. |
| Come across | Find by chance | I came across an interesting article. |

**Important:** Some expressions traditionally grouped with phrasal verbs, such as *look after* and *deal with*, are more precisely classified as prepositional verbs. For IELTS preparation, learning their meaning, grammar, and appropriate usage is more important than memorising these technical distinctions.

---

## 02. Speaking vs Academic Writing

Not every phrasal verb belongs in an academic essay.

| Expression | Speaking | Academic Writing |
|---|---|---|
| Figure out | Natural | Prefer *determine* or *understand* |
| Put off | Natural | Prefer *postpone* |
| Find out | Natural | Often prefer *discover* or *establish* |
| Carry out | Appropriate | Appropriate |
| Account for | Appropriate | Appropriate |
| Bring about | Appropriate | Appropriate |
| Point out | Appropriate | Usually appropriate |
| Cut down on | Natural | Often prefer *reduce* |
| Look into | Natural | Often prefer *investigate* |
| Give rise to | Appropriate | Appropriate |

> [!warning] IELTS Writing rule
> Don't automatically replace every phrasal verb with a single-word verb. Some multiword expressions, such as *account for*, *carry out*, and *give rise to*, are entirely suitable for formal academic prose.
>
> Choose the expression that communicates the exact meaning in the correct register.

## 03. Essential phrasal verbs for IELTS Speaking

### Education and learning

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Natural example |
|---|---|---|
| Keep up with | Maintain the same pace | I sometimes struggle to keep up with my coursework. |
| Catch up on | Complete missed work | I use weekends to catch up on my reading. |
| Fall behind | Make insufficient progress | Some students fall behind because they lack support. |
| Look up | Search for information | I look up unfamiliar words in a dictionary. |
| Go over | Review carefully | I usually go over my notes before an exam. |
| Take up | Start an activity | I took up reading English novels last year. |
| Drop out of | Leave a course before completion | Some students drop out of university for financial reasons. |
| Get through | Complete something difficult | I managed to get through a demanding course. |

**Speaking Part 1 example**

Question: *How do you usually prepare for exams?*

Answer:

"I normally go over my notes and work through practice questions. If I fall behind, I try to catch up over the weekend rather than leaving everything until the last minute."

**Why it works:** The expressions are relevant and natural. They explain an actual study routine without sounding memorised.

### Work and career

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Take on | Accept responsibility | I wouldn't want to take on too many responsibilities. |
| Set up | Establish | My uncle set up a small business. |
| Step down | Resign from a position | The manager stepped down last year. |
| Carry on | Continue | I carried on working despite the difficulties. |
| Take over | Assume control | A new director took over the company. |
| Turn down | Reject | She turned down a job offer. |
| Work out | Develop successfully | Fortunately, everything worked out in the end. |
| Deal with | Handle | Employees often deal with stressful situations. |

### Technology and the internet

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Log in | Access an account | I log in to my university portal every day. |
| Back up | Create a backup copy | I regularly back up important files. |
| Scroll through | Browse content | I sometimes scroll through social media before bed. |
| Cut down on | Reduce | I'm trying to cut down on screen time. |
| Keep up with | Stay informed about | It's difficult to keep up with technological developments. |
| Come across | Encounter unexpectedly | I came across a useful educational website. |
| Switch off | Turn off | I switch off notifications when I'm studying. |
| Rely on | Depend on | Many students rely on digital learning platforms. |

### Health and lifestyle

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Work out | Exercise | I try to work out three times a week. |
| Cut down on | Consume less | I've started cutting down on sugary drinks. |
| Give up | Stop a habit | My father gave up smoking years ago. |
| Take up | Begin a hobby | I recently took up swimming. |
| Stay up | Remain awake | I sometimes stay up late to finish assignments. |
| Get over | Recover from | It took me several days to get over the flu. |
| Slow down | Reduce speed or activity | People should slow down when they feel exhausted. |
| Wear out | Exhaust | Long working hours can wear people out. |

### Environment and society

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Cut down on | Reduce | Households should cut down on plastic waste. |
| Die out | Become extinct or disappear | Some traditional customs are gradually dying out. |
| Wipe out | Destroy completely | Severe flooding can wipe out entire communities. |
| Phase out | Remove gradually | Governments should phase out highly polluting technologies. |
| Bring about | Cause | Education can bring about positive social change. |
| Give rise to | Cause or produce | Rapid urbanisation can give rise to housing shortages. |
| Build up | Accumulate | Air pollution can build up in densely populated areas. |

## 04. Academic phrasal verbs worth mastering

These expressions are especially useful for discussing research, evidence, causes, changes, and solutions.

### A. Research and analysis

**1. Carry out — conduct**

Common collocations:
- carry out research
- carry out an investigation
- carry out an experiment
- carry out a survey

Example:

"The researchers carried out a survey involving 500 university students."

**2. Account for — explain or represent**

Common patterns:
- account for an increase
- account for a difference
- account for a proportion of the total

Examples:

"Several factors may account for the increase in unemployment."

"Transport accounted for 30% of total household expenditure."

These are two distinct meanings: *explain* and *constitute a proportion*.

**3. Point out — draw attention to**

"The report points out several weaknesses in the existing education system."

**4. Be based on — have something as a foundation**

"The conclusions are based on data collected over a ten-year period."

### B. Causes and consequences

**5. Bring about — cause a change**

"Investment in public transport could bring about significant improvements in urban mobility."

**6. Give rise to — produce or cause**

"Poor urban planning can give rise to serious environmental problems."

**7. Lead to — result in**

"Prolonged exposure to air pollution may lead to respiratory problems."

**8. Stem from — originate from**

"Many educational inequalities stem from differences in household income."

### C. Change and development

**9. Phase out — remove gradually**

"Several countries are gradually phasing out coal-fired power stations."

**10. Build up — accumulate**

"Excessive pressure can build up over time."

**11. Break down — separate into components or stop functioning**

"The data can be broken down into five age groups."

**12. Set out — present clearly**

"The report sets out several recommendations for improving public health."

## 05. Grammatical rules you must know

### Rule 1 — Separable phrasal verbs

Some phrasal verbs allow an object between the verb and particle.

Example:

"Turn off the computer."

"Turn the computer off."

Both are correct.

But with an object pronoun:

**Correct:** "Turn it off."

**Incorrect:** "Turn off it."

Other examples include *put off*, *pick up*, and *turn down* when used transitively.

### Rule 2 — Inseparable phrasal verbs

Some combinations cannot be separated.

**Correct:** "I look after my younger brother."

**Correct:** "I look after him."

**Incorrect:** "I look him after."

Other examples:
- rely on
- deal with
- come across
- look into

### Rule 3 — Three-part expressions

Examples:
- put up with
- look forward to
- run out of
- get away with
- keep up with

**Correct:** "I look forward to studying abroad."

**Incorrect:** "I look forward to study abroad."

The word *to* in *look forward to* is a preposition, so it can be followed by a noun or an -ing form.

### Rule 4 — Learn the complete pattern

Don't just memorise *depend*, *account*, or *give rise*.

Learn:

- depend **on** something
- account **for** something
- give rise **to** something
- cut down **on** something
- catch up **with** someone
- catch up **on** work
- look forward **to** doing something

> [!tip] Mnemonic: M-P-C-R
> **M**eaning → **P**attern → **C**ollocation → **R**egister
>
> If I know all four, I can use the expression confidently.

## 06. High-frequency mistakes

### Mistake 1 — Wrong preposition

**Incorrect:** The government should deal about pollution.

**Correct:** The government should deal with pollution.

### Mistake 2 — Wrong verb form

**Incorrect:** I look forward to meet new people.

**Correct:** I look forward to meeting new people.

### Mistake 3 — Incorrect word order

**Incorrect:** I turned off it.

**Correct:** I turned it off.

### Mistake 4 — Wrong meaning

**Incorrect:** I gave up my homework yesterday.

If the intended meaning is completing the homework, *give up* is inappropriate.

**Correct:** I finished my homework yesterday.

### Mistake 5 — Inappropriate register

**Too conversational for Academic Writing:**

"Governments should figure out how to fix this problem."

**More appropriate:**

"Governments should identify effective solutions to this problem."

### Mistake 6 — Forced vocabulary

**Unnatural:**

"The authorities should bring about a carrying out of educational activities."

**Better:**

"The authorities should implement educational programmes."

One precise verb is better than several poorly combined expressions.

## 07. IELTS Speaking practice

### Part 1 — Studies

**Question:** Do you enjoy studying?

Task: Give a natural answer using *keep up with* or *go over*.

### Part 2 — Describe a skill

Describe a skill you have learned.

You should say:
- What the skill is
- When you started learning it
- How you developed it
- Why it is useful

Suggested expressions, if relevant:
- take up
- work on
- pick up
- give up
- get through

Don't force all five into your answer.

### Part 3 — Education

**Question:** Why do some students struggle at university?

Possible vocabulary:
- fall behind
- keep up with
- drop out of
- rely on

Develop an actual argument rather than listing expressions.

## 08. Academic Writing practice

### Task 2 — Education

**Prompt:**

Some people believe universities should focus on practical skills rather than theoretical knowledge.

To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Practise these expressions where their meanings fit:
- be based on
- carry out
- lead to
- account for

**Example paragraph**

"Practical training can help students apply the knowledge they acquire at university. In subjects such as engineering and medicine, students often need to carry out experiments or complete supervised practical work. These activities can lead to a deeper understanding of theoretical concepts because learners are able to observe how principles operate in real situations."

**Why it works**

The language is appropriate for academic writing. The paragraph develops a single argument, and the multiword verbs contribute to the explanation without drawing attention to themselves.

## 09. Active recall exercises

### Exercise A — Fill in the blanks

1. Scientists carried ___ an experiment.
2. Some learners fall ___ when they miss classes.
3. The government plans to phase ___ older vehicles.
4. Poor nutrition can give rise ___ health problems.
5. I need to catch ___ on my assignments.

### Exercise B — Correct the mistakes

1. I am looking forward to visit London.
2. The researchers carried on an experiment.
3. She turned down it.
4. Many people rely in public transport.
5. The report is based in recent statistics.

### Exercise C — Rewrite more formally

1. We need to figure out why pollution is increasing.
2. The company put off the project.
3. The government should look into this issue.
4. Several problems popped up during the research.
5. The authorities should cut down on unnecessary expenditure.

### Exercise D — Speaking challenge

Record yourself answering:

1. How do you keep up with your studies?
2. Have you ever taken up a difficult hobby?
3. What habits should people give up?
4. How has technology brought about changes in education?

Review your recording for:
- Accuracy
- Naturalness
- Grammar
- Pronunciation
- Repetition

## Final principle

**I don't need to use more phrasal verbs. I need to use the right ones, correctly and naturally.**

My target is flexible English that works in the situation, not vocabulary memorised purely to impress an examiner.
