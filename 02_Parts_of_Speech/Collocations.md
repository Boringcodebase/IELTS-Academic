# IELTS Academic — Collocations Master Guide

*Learn natural word combinations. Understand their meaning. Use them accurately.*

> [!important] Core principle
> IELTS rewards flexible and precise vocabulary, not memorised lists of impressive expressions.
>
> **Meaning → Pattern → Context → Application → Correction**

## 01. What is a collocation?

A collocation is a combination of words that naturally occur together.

For example:

- **Make a decision** — natural
- **Do a decision** — incorrect
- **Heavy rain** — natural
- **Strong rain** — unnatural
- **Conduct research** — natural
- **Make research** — incorrect

Individual words may be grammatically correct while their combination sounds unnatural.

### Why collocations matter in IELTS

In Writing, collocations help communicate arguments, describe trends, and express relationships precisely.

In Speaking, they allow ideas to flow more naturally without repeatedly searching for individual words.

In Reading and Listening, recognising combinations can improve comprehension, particularly when questions paraphrase the original information.

**IELTS does not require a fixed number of collocations.** What matters is whether I can use an appropriate range accurately and flexibly.

## 02. Six essential collocation patterns

### Type 1 — Verb + noun

| Collocation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Make a decision | Choose between alternatives |
| Reach a conclusion | Arrive at a judgement |
| Conduct research | Investigate systematically |
| Gather evidence | Collect supporting information |
| Address a problem | Take action concerning an issue |
| Pose a threat | Present a danger |
| Play a role | Have a function or influence |
| Achieve a goal | Successfully reach an objective |
| Raise awareness | Increase public understanding |
| Take responsibility | Accept an obligation |
| Gain experience | Acquire practical knowledge |
| Meet a requirement | Satisfy a condition |
| Make progress | Improve or advance |
| Draw a distinction | Identify a difference |
| Allocate resources | Distribute available resources |

**Academic examples**

"Governments should allocate more resources to public education."

"Researchers need to gather reliable evidence before reaching a conclusion."

### Type 2 — Adjective + noun

| Collocation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Significant impact | Important or substantial effect |
| Widespread concern | Anxiety shared by many people |
| Substantial investment | Large financial commitment |
| Growing demand | Increasing need or desire |
| Serious consequences | Major negative results |
| Strong evidence | Convincing support |
| Sustainable development | Development balancing present and future needs |
| Equal opportunities | Fair chances of participation or success |
| Academic performance | Achievement in education |
| Public awareness | General knowledge of an issue |
| Long-term benefits | Advantages over an extended period |
| Rapid urbanisation | Fast growth of urban populations |
| Heavy traffic | Large volume of road vehicles |
| Chronic illness | Long-lasting medical condition |
| Renewable energy | Energy from replenishable sources |

**Academic example**

"Rapid urbanisation has created growing demand for affordable housing."

### Type 3 — Noun + noun

| Collocation | Example |
|---|---|
| Employment opportunities | Employment opportunities vary between regions. |
| Education system | The education system requires reform. |
| Government expenditure | Government expenditure increased. |
| Population growth | Population growth places pressure on infrastructure. |
| Carbon emissions | Carbon emissions need to be reduced. |
| Healthcare services | Healthcare services should be accessible. |
| Income inequality | Income inequality remains a social concern. |
| Traffic congestion | Traffic congestion affects commuters. |
| Research findings | Research findings support the hypothesis. |
| Consumer behaviour | Advertising can influence consumer behaviour. |
| Energy consumption | Energy consumption rises during winter. |
| Language proficiency | Language proficiency is important for international students. |

**Usage reminder**

Noun combinations have their own grammatical conventions.

For example:
- *research findings* — natural
- *researches findings* — incorrect

The first noun usually remains singular in combinations of this kind.

### Type 4 — Adverb + adjective

| Collocation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Highly effective | Very successful |
| Widely available | Accessible in many places |
| Deeply concerned | Very worried |
| Strongly opposed | Firmly against |
| Closely related | Having a strong connection |
| Increasingly important | Becoming more important |
| Particularly vulnerable | Especially exposed to harm |
| Economically viable | Financially practical |
| Socially acceptable | Accepted by society |
| Environmentally sustainable | Able to continue without unacceptable environmental harm |
| Fully aware | Completely informed |
| Severely disadvantaged | Experiencing major disadvantages |

**Example**

"Rural communities can be particularly vulnerable to shortages of healthcare professionals."

### Type 5 — Verb + adverb

| Collocation | Example |
|---|---|
| Increase significantly | Employment increased significantly. |
| Rise sharply | Prices rose sharply. |
| Decline gradually | Sales declined gradually. |
| Fall dramatically | Revenue fell dramatically. |
| Grow steadily | The population grew steadily. |
| Remain relatively stable | Costs remained relatively stable. |
| Vary considerably | Results vary considerably. |
| Differ substantially | The two groups differed substantially. |
| Strongly recommend | Experts strongly recommend regular exercise. |
| Widely recognise | The problem is widely recognised. |

**Important:** A dramatic expression must match the evidence. A change of 1% does not automatically justify *rose dramatically*.

### Type 6 — Word + preposition

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Access to | Access to quality education |
| Impact on | Impact on the environment |
| Increase in | Increase in unemployment |
| Decrease in | Decrease in expenditure |
| Demand for | Demand for affordable housing |
| Dependence on | Dependence on fossil fuels |
| Responsible for | Responsible for the outcome |
| Contribute to | Contribute to economic growth |
| Associated with | Associated with health risks |
| Relevant to | Relevant to the discussion |
| Similar to | Similar to previous results |
| Different from | Different from the earlier figure |
| Preference for | Preference for online learning |
| Solution to | Solution to the problem |
| Investment in | Investment in infrastructure |
| Benefit from | Benefit from improved services |

**Common errors**

Incorrect: *The impact in society is significant.*

Correct: *The impact on society is significant.*

Incorrect: *This contributes for economic growth.*

Correct: *This contributes to economic growth.*

## 03. Academic Writing Task 1 collocations

Task 1 vocabulary must match the actual information presented.

### A. Increases

| Collocation | Use |
|---|---|
| A sharp increase | Large, sudden growth |
| A gradual increase | Slow growth over time |
| A steady rise | Consistent upward movement |
| A significant increase | Substantial growth |
| An upward trend | Overall increase |
| Reach a peak | Attain the highest point |
| Rise considerably | Increase by a substantial amount |
| Experience growth | Show an increase |

**Examples**

"The number of international students experienced a steady rise between 2010 and 2020."

"Sales reached a peak of 80,000 units in 2018."

### B. Decreases

| Collocation | Use |
|---|---|
| A sharp decline | Large, sudden fall |
| A gradual decrease | Slow reduction |
| A downward trend | Overall decline |
| A substantial reduction | Large decrease |
| Fall steadily | Decrease consistently |
| Drop significantly | Fall by a substantial amount |
| Reach a low point | Fall to the lowest level |
| Experience a decline | Show a reduction |

**Example**

"The proportion of households without internet access declined steadily throughout the period."

### C. Stability and fluctuation

- Remain stable
- Remain relatively unchanged
- Level off
- Fluctuate considerably
- Experience minor fluctuations
- Show little variation
- Maintain a constant level
- Stabilise at a particular figure

**Example**

"After several years of growth, expenditure levelled off at approximately $50 million."

### D. Comparisons

| Collocation | Example |
|---|---|
| Higher than | The figure was higher than in 2010. |
| Lower than | Spending was lower than in the previous year. |
| Twice as high as | The rate was twice as high as that of Group B. |
| A considerable difference | There was a considerable difference between the groups. |
| A similar proportion | Both categories represented a similar proportion. |
| Account for the largest share | Food accounted for the largest share of expenditure. |
| Represent a small minority | This category represented a small minority of responses. |
| By contrast | By contrast, the second group experienced growth. |

### E. Graph-specific combinations

**Maps**
- Undergo significant development
- Expand residential areas
- Convert farmland into housing
- Construct new facilities
- Replace existing buildings
- Improve transport links

**Processes**
- Undergo a series of stages
- Be transported to
- Pass through a filter
- Be converted into
- Be stored in a container
- Produce the final product

**Pie charts**
- Account for a proportion
- Constitute a share
- Represent the majority
- Make up a small percentage
- Comprise several categories

### Task 1 practice

Use the following invented data:

- 2010: 20,000 visitors
- 2015: 35,000 visitors
- 2020: 70,000 visitors

Write two accurate sentences using:
- steady rise or significant increase
- more than triple
- reached

**Possible response**

"The number of visitors increased significantly from 20,000 in 2010 to 70,000 in 2020. By the end of the period, the figure had more than tripled."

The choice of vocabulary must reflect the actual chart. With only three data points, a claim of continuous or steady growth between the dates would require caution.

## 04. Academic Writing Task 2 — Functional collocations

These expressions are useful across many essay topics.

### A. Presenting problems

- Face serious challenges
- Pose a threat to
- Create difficulties
- Place pressure on
- Raise concerns about
- Have negative consequences
- Widen the gap between
- Exacerbate existing inequalities
- Contribute to a problem
- Become a pressing issue

**Example**

"Rapid population growth can place considerable pressure on public infrastructure."

### B. Presenting solutions

- Address the underlying causes
- Implement effective measures
- Introduce stricter regulations
- Develop a long-term strategy
- Allocate additional funding
- Improve public access
- Provide financial incentives
- Raise public awareness
- Encourage responsible behaviour
- Promote equal opportunities

**Example**

"Governments could introduce financial incentives to encourage businesses to adopt cleaner technologies."

### C. Explaining causes and consequences

- Be driven by
- Stem from
- Result from
- Lead to
- Result in
- Give rise to
- Have a direct impact on
- Play a significant role in
- Be closely linked to
- Produce unintended consequences

**Important distinction**

- **Result from** introduces a cause.
- **Result in** introduces an effect.

"Poor health may result from inadequate nutrition."

"Inadequate nutrition may result in poor health."

### D. Advantages and disadvantages

- Offer considerable benefits
- Provide greater flexibility
- Improve overall efficiency
- Create new opportunities
- Have long-term advantages
- Present potential risks
- Involve substantial costs
- Have unintended consequences
- Outweigh the disadvantages
- Strike a balance between

**Example**

"Although remote work offers greater flexibility, it can also create difficulties for communication and teamwork."

### E. Arguments and evaluation

- Provide convincing evidence
- Challenge a common assumption
- Support the argument
- Acknowledge the limitations
- Consider the implications
- Draw a distinction between
- Reach a reasonable conclusion
- Take a balanced approach
- Assess the effectiveness of
- Question the assumption that

**Example**

"It is important to distinguish between providing equal opportunities and guaranteeing equal outcomes."

## 08. Precision: words that are not interchangeable

### Economic vs economical

**Economic growth** — growth related to the economy.

**Economical transport** — transport that is inexpensive or efficient to operate.

Incorrect: *The country experienced economical growth.*

Correct: *The country experienced economic growth.*

### Effective vs efficient

**Effective measures** achieve their intended result.

**Efficient measures** achieve results with minimal wasted resources.

A policy can be effective without being efficient.

### Significant vs dramatic

**Significant increase** can mean a substantial or meaningful increase.

**Dramatic increase** implies an especially striking or sudden increase.

The two expressions are not always interchangeable.

### A number of vs an amount of

**A number of students** — countable plural noun.

**An amount of money** — uncountable noun.

### Raise vs rise

**Raise awareness** — transitive verb, requiring an object.

**Rise in prices** — intransitive verb.

Correct:
- Governments should raise awareness.
- Prices rose considerably.

Incorrect:
- Governments should rise awareness.

### Affect vs effect

**Have an effect on**

**Affect something**

Correct:
- Pollution affects public health.
- Pollution has an effect on public health.

## 09. Collocations in actual IELTS paragraphs

### Task 2 example — Environment

**Question**

Some people believe governments should take primary responsibility for protecting the environment, while others think individuals should be responsible.

Discuss both views and give your opinion.

**Model paragraph**

"Governments have an important role to play in environmental protection because they can introduce regulations that affect entire industries. For example, stricter emissions standards may encourage manufacturers to invest in cleaner technologies. Such measures can bring about meaningful reductions in industrial pollution, particularly when they are supported by effective monitoring and enforcement."

**Useful collocations**

- play an important role
- environmental protection
- introduce regulations
- emissions standards
- invest in cleaner technologies
- meaningful reductions
- industrial pollution
- effective monitoring

**Why the paragraph works**

The expressions support a coherent argument. They are not inserted merely to increase vocabulary complexity.

### Task 2 example — Education

"Access to quality education can improve employment prospects and contribute to social mobility. Students from disadvantaged backgrounds may face financial barriers that limit their opportunities to pursue higher education. Providing targeted financial assistance could help reduce these inequalities and allow more students to develop valuable skills."

**Useful collocations**

- access to quality education
- improve employment prospects
- contribute to social mobility
- disadvantaged backgrounds
- financial barriers
- pursue higher education
- targeted financial assistance

>
