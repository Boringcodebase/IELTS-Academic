# IELTS Mastery Vault

*My IELTS preparation headquarters.*

**Target: Band 8.0+ | Approach: IELTS Advantage**

This vault is where I study English, practise IELTS questions, review my mistakes, and measure my progress.

The objective is straightforward: improve the skills that IELTS actually assesses.

I don't need hundreds of memorised phrases, complicated essay templates, or an impressive collection of grammar notes. I need to understand each task, produce accurate English, and correct the weaknesses that affect my score.

> [!important] The core method
> **Learn → Practise → Analyse → Improve → Retest**
>
> Every study session should contribute to at least one of these stages.

## 02. Start with a diagnosis

Before starting another course, I need to know what is preventing me from reaching my target.

### Initial assessment

1. Complete a timed Listening test.
2. Complete a timed Reading test.
3. Write one Academic Task 1 response and one Task 2 essay.
4. Record a complete Speaking practice test.
5. Review the results against the IELTS assessment criteria.

Listening and Reading can be evaluated using answer keys. Writing and Speaking require criterion-based assessment; a self-estimated band is not an official result.

### Understand the problem

| Problem | What to investigate |
|---|---|
| Low Writing Task Response | Unanswered questions or insufficient development |
| Weak Coherence and Cohesion | Unclear progression or poorly connected ideas |
| Limited Lexical Resource | Repetition, inaccurate word choice, weak collocations |
| Grammar errors | Frequent mistakes or limited sentence control |
| Speaking hesitation | Difficulty developing ideas or expressing them spontaneously |
| Reading mistakes | Misinterpreted questions, weak evidence identification |

## 03. Writing Task 2 — Think, write, check

**Main principle:** The examiner evaluates how effectively I address the question and use English, not how intellectually complicated my opinions sound.

### Stage 1 — Think

Before writing:

- Identify the question type.
- Understand every part of the prompt.
- Decide on my position where required.
- Select relevant ideas that I can explain.
- Plan the logical progression of my essay.

**IDEA → EXPLANATION → EXAMPLE**

For each main body paragraph:

1. **Idea:** State a clear, relevant point.
2. **Explanation:** Explain why or how the point supports my argument.
3. **Example:** Illustrate the point with a specific, relevant example when useful.

An effective paragraph develops one clear argument. Adding more ideas without explaining them usually makes the writing weaker.

### Stage 2 — Write

Use an appropriate structure for the question.

**Introduction**
- Introduce the specific issue accurately.
- Answer the question and establish the essay's direction.

**Body paragraphs**
- Begin with a clear controlling idea.
- Explain the argument.
- Provide relevant support.
- Maintain a logical connection to the question.

**Conclusion**
- Summarise the central answer or position.
- Avoid introducing a new argument.

### Stage 3 — Check

Review the response for:

- [ ] Task completion and relevance
- [ ] Clear position where required
- [ ] Adequate explanation and support
- [ ] Logical paragraph progression
- [ ] Word choice and collocations
- [ ] Articles, agreement, tense, and punctuation
- [ ] Spelling and unnecessary repetition

**Suggested 40-minute practice allocation**

| Stage | Time |
|---|---|
| Think and plan | 5–10 min |
| Write | 25–30 min |
| Check | About 5 min |

## 04. Academic Writing Task 1 — Select, compare, report

Task 1 is not about describing every number or using as many trend expressions as possible.

The goal is to summarise the most important information accurately.

### My method

**Understand → Select → Organise → Report → Check**

1. Understand the visual and its units.
2. Identify the main features and overall patterns.
3. Select relevant comparisons.
4. Organise the information logically.
5. Report the details accurately.

### Task 1 essentials

- [ ] Clear introduction
- [ ] Accurate overview
- [ ] Relevant key features
- [ ] Meaningful comparisons
- [ ] Correct figures, units, and time references
- [ ] Appropriate vocabulary and grammar

For Academic Task 1, practise completing the response in approximately 20 minutes and writing at least 150 words.

## 05. Speaking — Natural communication

My goal is to speak fluently, accurately, and spontaneously.

I should prepare for the types of questions in the exam, but I shouldn't memorise complete answers and try to force them into unrelated questions.

### Part 1 — Direct answers

Practise:
- Answering the actual question
- Adding a relevant detail
- Giving a short explanation when appropriate

### Part 2 — Extended speaking

Practise:
- Organising ideas quickly
- Speaking for an extended period
- Using examples and details
- Maintaining a natural pace
- Recovering when I lose my train of thought

### Part 3 — Developing discussion

Practise:
- Expressing opinions
- Explaining reasons
- Comparing alternatives
- Considering consequences
- Discussing abstract ideas

### Speaking feedback checklist

| Criterion | What to review |
|---|---|
| Fluency and Coherence | Flow, hesitation, repetition, idea development |
| Lexical Resource | Range, appropriacy, paraphrasing, collocations |
| Grammatical Range and Accuracy | Flexibility, sentence control, errors |
| Pronunciation | Intelligibility, stress, rhythm, individual sounds |

### My practice loop

- [ ] Record a response
- [ ] Listen without reading a transcript
- [ ] Identify unnecessary pauses and repetition
- [ ] Examine my grammar and vocabulary choices
- [ ] Obtain pronunciation feedback from the recording
- [ ] Repeat with improvements, without memorising a script

## 06. Reading — Follow the evidence

Reading performance depends on accurate comprehension, question analysis, and time management.

### My approach

**Understand the question → Locate evidence → Analyse meaning → Choose the answer**

For every incorrect response, determine whether I:

- Misunderstood the question
- Failed to locate relevant information
- Misinterpreted paraphrasing
- Confused False with Not Given
- Missed the paragraph's main idea
- Lost time using an ineffective approach

### Resources

- [[Reading_TFNG_Drills]]
- [[Reading_Matching_Headings_Strategy]]

**Review rule:** Don't simply check the correct letter. Find the evidence that justifies the correct answer and explains why my answer was wrong.

## 07. Listening — Understand, predict, verify

Listening is not just about recognising individual words.

I need to follow meaning while processing natural speech, paraphrases, distractors, and details.

### Before listening

- Read the instructions.
- Identify the required answer format.
- Predict the type of information needed.
- Notice keywords and possible paraphrases.

### During listening

- Follow the speaker's meaning.
- Watch for corrections and changes of mind.
- Move on if an answer is missed.
- Avoid letting one mistake affect the next questions.

### After listening

- Check the answer key.
- Locate the missed information in the transcript.
- Replay the relevant section.
- Identify the actual reason for the mistake.
- Practise the same listening difficulty again.

### Resources

- [[Listening_Sound_Patterns]]
- [[Listening_Spelling_Predictor]]

**Reminder:** Replaying an entire recording without analysing the mistakes is less useful than studying the exact section I misunderstood.

## 09. The error correction system

This is where practice becomes improvement.

### My five-step correction process

**1. Problem**

Identify the specific weakness.

**2. Evidence**

Keep the original sentence, response, or test question.

**3. Why**

Explain the reason for the mistake.

**4. Fix**

Write the correct version and understand the underlying rule.

**5. Practice**

Apply the correction in new contexts.

### Example

**Original:** *The government should make more researches.*

**Problem:** Incorrect use of the uncountable noun *research*.

**Correction:** *The government should conduct more research.*

**Practice:** Write three sentences using *conduct research*, *research findings*, and *scientific research*.

### Error review checklist

- [ ] Understand the correction
- [ ] Create original examples
- [ ] Review after one day
- [ ] Review again after three days
- [ ] Apply the structure in a new IELTS task

**A mistake is not resolved until I can avoid it independently.**

**Don't practise just to finish a task. Practise to find out what needs improving.**

If I understand it, I should correct it.

If I correct it, I should test myself again.
